# Narrative

## The Scientist

A researcher working for a company funding memory alteration and transfer research. The project ballooned in budget and was cut. The scientist, too obsessed to stop, locked himself away and made it a personal project — using himself as the test subject.

The machine corrupts the memories it transfers and he's trying to debug it. In the process, repressed memories surface: his own failures and blunders are recreated inside the machine as worlds. The machine generates a proxy character — a young boy resembling his childhood friend — to inhabit those worlds.

Each time the scientist uploads himself to debug further, more memories are introduced that help the boy progress.

**Current state:** The scientist doesn't want to remember. He is preparing a noose. The player — under the assumption they need to save the dream world — is driven to find the truth through the scientist in order to progress.

## The Man (John Smith)

The person the Dimension Engine is currently modeling worlds from. Late 40s, experiencing a dying scenario that led him to agree to be a test subject. All worlds are generated from his psyche; NPCs are based on memories of people or concepts that impacted him most.

The Man's backstory has **major plot branches**. How the player resolves each branch determines how The Man lived his life — not retroactively, but revealing what was always true. At the end of the game, the scientist(s) can confirm what the player has pieced together.

### Open Questions for The Man
- What kind of story do we want to tell with this guy?
- Is there a core lifestyle or personality to explore?
- What kind of people affected him enough to end up in the simulation?
- How deep/dark does this get? Secrets? Murder? Depression?
- At what point does the player realize they aren't in control of the story — that some things have already concluded in real life?

### Major Story Branches

**Porcelain Girl Breaking**
- Healing her → the relationship was saved.
- Letting her break → the relationship was lost.
- Her degradation may serve as a gauge for which ending the player is heading toward.

## Themes

- Memory, regret, and the things people choose to forget.
- The cycle of forcing others through the same pain you experienced.
- Identity — corrupted programs developing personhood, angels who don't know what they believe in.
- Patience and acceptance: you cannot save every NPC. Their journey is their own.
