# NPCs

Most NPCs can be simple set dressing with only a few lines. Complexity is reserved for recurring characters.

---

## Recurring NPCs

### Porcelain Girl
A cracked, fragile doll. Found in **Shelf Top World** (a world of large clock structures, picture frames, books, plushies, toys, etc.). She insists she's not in her own world and needs to get home.

**Joining the party:**
She doesn't trust the player at first. Give her a specific item and she'll let you carry her as a **Porcelain Doll** inventory item. From there she can comment on events whenever the developers wish.

**Personality:** Distrustful and guarded at first — not abrasive, just not warm. Opens up over time, eventually yapping about current events and showing concern when something weird happens to the player.

**Degradation mechanic:**
Porcelain Girl slowly degrades over the course of the game. If not repaired before reaching her home world, she becomes too fractured to travel and must stay there. She makes comments about her degradation — lamenting it and time — but ultimately admits it's not the player's fault, attributing it to *something else*.

**How to fix her (proposed):**
Fixing her may involve helping other NPCs who represent people in The Man's life. She slowly heals as the player heals other characters — tying her condition to the game's branching story state. She becomes a clear gauge for which ending the player is heading toward.

**Her world:**
A cozy drawing of a house with flowers. Brightly lit if Porcelain Girl is intact; dim if she is broken.

### Angel (tentative name)
A beautiful angelic being representing spirituality — or at least the innocent idea of it. Calm and gentle in appearance; presents as all-knowing but cannot answer basic questions about the religion they belong to. They don't know what they believe in, or even what they are.

When confronted with this fact, Angel embarks on a **fake-it-till-you-make-it self-discovery arc** — becoming highly expressive out of character (large surprised eyes, sweating, nervous energy). Their secret home can eventually be found; they'll try to nervously explain away a closet full of replacement wings.

### Bongo Scongo — The Train Conductor
Shitty ex-car salesman. His spouse divorced him. He took up chainsmoking but can't smoke in the conductor's cabin.

**Sidequest:**
- Bongo asks for cigarettes if the player finds them.
- Giving him cigarettes crashes the train into **Addiction World**.
- In Addiction World, the player finds Bongo's spouse and convinces them to snap Bongo out of his funk.
- After the spouse intervenes, Bongo regains his nerve and swears off smoking.
- Giving Bongo cigarettes *again* crashes the train into Addiction World once more. This time, Bongo is permanently removed as conductor. The player must conduct the train themselves.

**First encounter on the train (before sidequest):**
> "Pressure's good. Speed's good. Yup… all smooth."
> "A passenger! What are you doing up here?"
> "Oh, just exploring, are you?"
> "Be careful — I've just gotten her all keyed up. The slightest imbalance and... POW!!"
> "Haha, don't look so scared! I'm an expert at this."
> "Wait a minute. I don't remember seeing you board at the station. Where's your TICKET STUB?"

If the player claims to have one: "That's not a TICKET STUB! That's just pocket lint! No freeloaders on the train!" — emergency stop, transfer to Platform World.
If the player admits they don't: "I appreciate your honesty. You're a good kid." — same result.

### Mice (general)
Multiple mice NPCs hidden in the tiny corners and hidden-away spaces of different worlds. Outside observers who offer quips and quirks about whatever world they inhabit.

Possibly smaller observational programs within the simulation — less capable of interacting with it than the player, more like viewers than inhabitants.

## World-Specific NPCs

### Medical Student / Nurse (Body World)
One of The Man's friends, someone he looked up to, manifested as a nurse or doctor. Struggling to keep Body World alive against disease and corruption. Stressed, overworked, and losing the battle.

Possible backstory tie: The Man was once in medical school but left for some extenuating circumstance. He may have agreed to the program partly because of his lifelong love of science and health — and his complicated feelings about leaving medicine.

---

### The Clerk (Platform World)
Ticket booth attendant. Head always obscured by hat. Idle: shuffling papers endlessly. The OPEN sign next to the booth is key. No useful dialogue — interacting produces only garbled static.

Upon returning with a Ticket Stub: the Clerk freezes, the OPEN sign shuts off, he stands — revealing no head beneath the hat.

---

### The Orphan (Platform World)
A small child sitting on the platform edge, kicking their feet. Turns to look at the player on interaction, then returns to doing nothing. After 30 seconds of standing beside them, walks away to BOTTOM TRACK and vanishes, leaving a Ticket Stub.

---

### The Waiting Man (Platform World)
A forlorn man staring down the train tunnel. Interacting makes a faint rustling sound. Gradually degrades in posture across visits (STANDING → SLOUCHING → FALLING OVER), eventually dropping a Ticket Stub.

---

### DROWNED (The Wrecked Car, Platform World)
A half-submerged person slumped over and dripping inside the Wrecked Car. Interacting triggers their disappearance on the next visit to the car. A suitcase containing the **CONDENSATION CHARM** floats outside after they've been interacted with. If never interacted with, they remain permanently.

## Train Intro NPCs (on the initial train ride)

**NPC 1** — Excited about having saved up for a Ticket Stub. Sympathizes with the player's lack of one but can't help directly.
> "Sounds like you're going to be in trouble… Don't let THE CONDUCTOR catch you!"
> (on repeat visits) "You're pretty talkative, eh? Maybe if we hadn't met on this train, we could have been friends. But alas, that's part of being a traveler… You meet people and move on. I'll remember you though, my teensy weensy friend!"

**NPC 2** — Relaxed traveler who advocates for slowing down and enjoying the scenery. Makes up increasingly absurd cloud shapes (mallet, mallard, "mallon") before admitting he didn't actually see any of them.
> "Life can be nice when you stop to admire it."
> (later) "I'll be honest, I… didn't really see any of those things before. Please don't despise me, okay?"
