# General Premise

Scientists created a Dimension engine (tentative name) that allows worlds to be generated from within a machine based on a person's memories. # Game Mechanics

## Main game mechanics that are consistent across worlds.

Train Bells \- Each world has a bell that allows the player to fast travel to that world. Upon interacting, the player unlocks the world on their map. The ‘entrance’ to the train can be anything, from a door in a hallway, to a hole in the ground.

Train Whistle \- Item the player can use anywhere to immediately shunt back to the train. Does not create an animation or anything, but it will play a short whistle sound effect and have an animated transition before placing the player back in the train itself.

Keypad \- Enter codes into the keypad, then hit enter to transfer worlds.

- The keypad has certain buttons missing the player has to find.  
- The keypad has extra esoteric options that are hidden behind secret codes or items. Such as a key opening to a second set of buttons labeled with symbols rather than numbers.  
- Blacklight hidden ink on the keypad?  
- Perhaps a 9 is missing and in order to get to an early world you need a 9, and you eventually find a way to turn yourself/the numpad upside down and then you can just press the 6 (now 9\) and go into that new world. Then you can get a number 9 from that world so that you don’t have to worry about doing that every time.   
- The Hidden Keypad: Astrology Symbols? (Use our own symbols)  
  - 1 Neptune, 2 Uranus, 3 Saturn, 4 Jupiter, 5 Mars, 6 Sun, 7 Venus, 8 Mercury, 9 Moon, 0 Earth  
  - Hidden Keypad columns are out of order? 897, 564, 312, 0

There are flags in place to keep players from progressing too quickly upon new game. For instance, they cannot enter the final world right away from starting a new game due to needing specific items, such as missing numbers on the keypad, specific items (such as Effects from YN), or even omniscient narration keeping you from progressing further.

There is a map on the train depicting the worlds in a tower-like structure. As the player finds each code for each area, it will automatically fill in on the map.  Selecting a POI (point of interest) will set the code in the number pad, and you will hit enter to confirm.Touching the map allows the player to select a world and auto-transport to that world, but you can still use the keypad to enter the world manually. This would allow for special easter egg or secret worlds while keeping players from having to memorize every number on a map just a few feet away.

# Platform World/Demo Writeup by Mark\!\!

The first area of the game. This is the start, and is where the player finds their first item: the **TICKET STUB**. The goal of Platform World is to introduce the player to certain themes and mechanics such as patience, interaction, experimentation, and non-linear thinking. This could serve as the demo. I don’t know. There are four sub-sections: **Layout**, **NPCs**, **Radiant Events**, and **SOGGY BOOTS**.

**Layout:** Platform World is an infinitely looping train platform. Instead of train tracks, there is dark water which the trains can somehow still skim the surface of. To the left and right, the player can continue on and on forever. At the top of the screen is a stairway which will lead to the opposite side of the tracks. There is not an “upper platform” so to speak–the player simply shifts to the opposite side of the tracks. This is important for a method of collecting a ticket stub. For the purpose of this writeup, I will refer to the map in terms of TOP TRACK and BOTTOM TRACK.  
TOP TRACK is where the player starts, directly facing a ticket booth or something along those lines. An NPC inside the booth can be interacted with, but they won’t offer anything helpful. **THE CLERK** (rough sketch of portrait to the right) won’t say anything to the player. Interacting with them will make a garbled static noise and that’s all. However, there is room for further interaction once the player gets a **TICKET STUB**.  
TOP TRACK also has a couple of other NPCs, including **THE ORPHAN** and **THE WAITING MAN**. These two NPCs are related to the quest to get the **TICKET STUB**, but I will cover them later.  
The player can only move so far down before they are stopped by the end of the platform. However, there *ARE* stairs on the end of the platform (just before it loops) where the player can descend onto the waterlogged tracks themselves.  
If the player goes up the stairs, they can find themselves on the BOTTOM TRACK, which is devoid of any NPCs or interactable phenomena. However, it is still important as a method of getting a **TICKET STUB**. Functionally, BOTTOM TRACK is the same as TOP TRACK except flipped.  
Every real-world minute, a train will pull into the station and allow the player to attempt to board. If they have a **TICKET STUB**, they will be welcomed onto the tracks and everything will proceed as normal. If they do not have a **TICKET STUB**, they will be barred entry and must collect one before returning. The train will also come based on NPC interactions, as detailed below.  
There is a secret area in Platform World only reachable if you use the **SOGGY BOOTS** item. It takes players to a crashed train car which, when entered, leads to special interactions. It is noted in its own section.

**NPCS**: There are three NPCs in Platform World.

1) **THE CLERK** \- An attendant for the platform. His idle animation involves his hand passing over the desk, grabbing some papers, then sliding them the other way before repeating again. He keeps his head down and his face obscured, which means his hat and part of his uniform are all the player can see. There is a sign next to the booth that reads OPEN, which is important for interactions. No matter how much the player tries, though, they won’t get anything important out of **THE CLERK**.   
   1) If the player returns to **THE CLERK** with a **TICKET STUB**, then **THE CLERK** will freeze in place and no longer perform his idle animation. After a few seconds, the OPEN sign will shut off. **THE CLERK** will also stand up to reveal there is no head beneath his hat, shown right.   
   2) If the player gives the **TICKET STUB** to **THE CLERK** via a text box choice, then he will punch it and make it useless. The player will not be able to board the train afterwards and will have to find a different **TICKET STUB**. This can only happen once, though, to avoid any frustration.  
2) **THE ORPHAN** \- A small child who sits on the edge of the platform, kicking their feet aimlessly. Interacting with the child will not do anything, but they will turn to look at the player before returning to their regularly scheduled routine of nothing. If the player stands beside **THE ORPHAN** for about 30 seconds without interrupting them, then **THE ORPHAN** will get up from their seat and walk away. If the player follows **THE ORPHAN**, they proceed to the stairs as if they are moving to the BOTTOM TRACK. If the player follows them there, **THE ORPHAN** will not be visible on the BOTTOM TRACK and will have vanished from the game. Returning to **THE ORPHAN’S** original position yields a **TICKET STUB**.  
   1) If the player finds a **TICKET STUB** some other way and tries to give it to **THE ORPHAN**, then the train will stop at the station. **THE ORPHAN** will try to board the train, but will be told no by the attendants, leaving **THE ORPHAN** to walk away towards BOTTOM TRACK as usual. The **TICKET STUB** will not be dropped, and the player will have to find another one as the train will leave after this event.  
3) **THE WAITING MAN** \- A forlorn-looking man who stares off into the ever-looping distance of the train tunnel. Interacting with **THE WAITING MAN** produces no effect, just a faint rustling noise. Returning to **THE WAITING MAN** after visiting other screens or talking to other NPCs will make his posture change. He goes from STANDING to SLOUCHING to FALLING OVER in front of the player. Once he falls over, he drops a **TICKET STUB** in front of him. If the player picks it up, **THE WAITING MAN** will get back to his feet and walk into the water via the stairway, disappearing forever. If the player waits a little bit, **THE WAITING MAN** will grab the **TICKET STUB** he lost and the train will arrive. Although he tries to board, the attendants refuse him passage, and **THE WAITING MAN** drops his **TICKET STUB** for the player to pick up as the train leaves.  
   1) If the player tries to give **THE WAITING MAN** a spare **TICKET STUB** before his event happens, the train will stop and **THE WAITING MAN** will attempt to board as normal. He will once again be spurned and will drop the **TICKET STUB** as the train leaves, then will walk into the water, disappearing.

Common threads between NPCs: Patience is key\! Furthermore, you cannot save them or get them onto the train. Their journey is their own, and you can’t influence that. That’s okay, since you can get onto the train yourself and proceed anyway.

**TICKET STUB Methods:** There are three ways to get a **TICKET STUB**.

1) From **THE ORPHAN** NPC, as detailed above.  
2) From **THE WAITING MAN** NPC, as detailed above.  
3) If the player stands on BOTTOM TRACK, on the boarding platform, just as the train passes by, then a **TICKET STUB** will appear next to them for pickup. This is sort of secretive, but I think it fits with the idea of a Yume Nikki-like.  
4) If the player goes into the water via the stairwell, the screen will darken and their model will sink into the water. After a moment, the player is submerged entirely and the screen goes black. Once a few seconds have passed, the player is on TOP TRACK again and will have a **DAMAGED TICKET STUB** which leads them to an alternate version of the train. Returning to the water after this has no mechanical effect.

**Radiant Events:** Some radiant, chance-based events that can happen a la Yume Nikki.

1) **Station Announcement**: A garbled, gibberish audio clip can play, reminiscent to an announcement at a train station. Further percent chance for the clip to cut off mid-play and lead to total silence for a few seconds before things return to normal.  
2) **Liquid Lag**: The player’s reflection in the water lags behind them for a few seconds. If the player turns or stops, the reflection will continue walking offscreen and will be gone until the cell is reloaded or switched via exiting to BOTTOM TRACK and back again.  
3) **Ticket Punched**: The sound of a ticket being punched echoes down the platform. If the player can see **THE CLERK**, their animation pauses for a moment before continuing on again.  
4) **Repositioning**: **THE ORPHAN** will get up from their seat, move a few paces, and sit down again. If this happens three times, the player is guaranteed the extra **TICKET STUB** from this NPC.   
5) **Incorrect Announcement**: An announcement plays, similarly to Station Announcement, but it references a code to a world that doesn’t exist. Inputting the code in the train will lead somewhere unexpected.  
6) **Platform Without You**: After entering the cell again via traveling to BOTTOM TRACK or TOP TRACK, the player character will not be visible, but footsteps will still sound. The audio will be muffled and pitched down, but after 5 seconds, the player returns to the screen and everything is normal again.  
7) **Water Walker**: If the player is on BOTTOM TRACK, they may see footsteps appearing on the water’s surface for a moment before they disappear. Entering the water after this point will grant the player a unique item, the **SOGGY BOOTS**, which will let them traverse on top of the water to reach a secret area.
